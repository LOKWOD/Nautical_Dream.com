Fourth Lake by Boat: An Inlet Family Day With the First Turn Kept Close

A hard-surface ramp and room for trailers make access legible, not the whole lake predictable. Prove the crew, boat and weather close to the Inlet launch before adding distance.

Editorial disclosure: The header image is an original AI-assisted editorial illustration, not documentary photography of Fourth Lake or a named launch. Official access information was reviewed September 27, 2026. Water level, weather, parking, docks and posted rules can change; verify them directly before towing.

By Nautical Dream Editorial DeskUpdated September 27, 202617 minute read

NYSDEC's current Hamilton County launch directory lists Fourth Lake in the Town of Inlet at 43.754364° N, 74.792441° W: a hard-surface launch with parking for 25 cars and trailers and an accessible designation. Those are useful planning facts, not a promise of an empty lot, a dock, a particular ramp depth or gentle water. This family plan makes the launch the return anchor, keeps the first operating loop close, and treats any farther exploration as an earned option rather than the definition of a successful day.

Use the exact state listing as the plan base

Save the NYSDEC Hamilton County launch page and coordinates offline. Confirm that navigation is leading to the Fourth Lake listing in Inlet, not another access on the connected Fulton Chain or a private marina. The state record says hard surface, 25 cars and trailers, and accessible; it does not promise a boarding dock, launch fee status, fuel, toilets or overnight parking.

Read current signs on arrival and ask DEC Region 5 about unclear access details before the trip. Walk the ramp, turnaround and staging area before backing. Use the ramp evaluation guide with the actual tow vehicle and trailer, not a generic satellite image.

Separate launch capability from lake capability

DEC defines a hard-surface launch as one that affords float-off and float-on launching for most trailered boats. That classification does not guarantee sufficient depth for every draft, low water, bunk geometry or axle configuration. Inspect grade, edge, traction, crosswind, traffic and recovery room.

If the tow vehicle must enter farther than its manual allows, the trailer drops beyond the visible surface, or the crew cannot board without unsafe climbing, stop. A nominally capable ramp is not a command to launch. The launch checklist should produce a clear go or no-go before the boat leaves the trailer.

Make the first loop a systems check

After clearing the ramp at idle speed and observing posted zones, stay close enough that a slow return remains uncomplicated. Verify steering, throttle and shift response, cooling-water indications, gauges, bilge condition, battery state, radio or phone coverage, crew seating and life-jacket fit. Look back at the launch so every capable adult can identify the return point.

This is not dead time. A fuel smell, alarm, weak steering, unfamiliar vibration, rising bilge water or uncomfortable passenger ends the outing early. The predeparture checklist happens before launch; the proof loop tests the system under real load without borrowing distance.

Let observed wind limit the day

Check the current National Weather Service forecast, radar and any applicable lake or regional observations before departure. Then compare them with flags, tree movement, cloud development and the actual water. Fourth Lake can be calm near shore while a longer fetch builds a different return.

Choose a family-specific wind, wave, temperature and thunderstorm limit before launching. Set a turn time that preserves daylight and a slow reserve. If wind direction would make the return harder, shorten the extension. Thunder, darkening water, rapidly changing wind or a marine warning means return or remain ashore; do not wait for rain to validate the decision.

Keep navigation conservative on a connected chain

A charted connection does not make every passage suitable for every boat, water level or operator. Use current official charts and local notices, maintain a proper lookout and respect markers, no-wake areas, swimmers, paddlers and shore activity. Do not follow another boat through a route whose depth, clearance or traffic pattern you have not verified.

For a first family day, staying on Fourth Lake is a complete plan. A connected-lake mileage goal adds unfamiliar passages and a more complicated return. Add it only when the operator has current local information, the boat fits, and the weather and crew still have wide margin.

Give children jobs that do not compete with the helm

Every child wears a correctly fitted life jacket and remains in a designated seat while the boat is underway. One adult operates; another manages children, lines, snacks and lookout duties. Assign simple jobs such as watching for the launch marker, reporting floating debris or checking the time.

Keep hands inside near docks and other boats. No child handles a line under load or stands between the boat and dock. Pack water, food, sun protection, warm layers and dry clothing so a basic comfort problem does not push the operator toward an unplanned landing.

Do not turn a shoreline into assumed access

Wooded or quiet shore does not automatically mean public landing, swimming, picnicking or toilet access. Use only places where current public rules and signs clearly allow the intended activity. Respect private property, wildlife, wakes and sanitation requirements.

Keep the family plan self-contained. If a child needs a break, the verified launch and vehicle are the default return. The absence of a documented public stop is a reason to shorten the route, not to improvise a landing on an attractive beach or dock.

Clean, drain and dry at the lawful point

New York requires boaters to follow Clean, Drain, Dry practices before launching into public waters. Remove visible plants and animals, drain water as required and arrive with the boat, trailer and gear clean. Follow posted decontamination or steward instructions.

After retrieval, move fully clear of the ramp before completing tie-downs and inspection. Drain only where lawful, never move aquatic material to another water, and inspect bunks, rollers, propeller, lower unit, anchor and lines. The busy-ramp etiquette guide keeps the inspection distinct from the active launch lane.

Recover while the ramp still feels easy

Return before the lot, light, wind or family patience becomes the controlling factor. Assign one vehicle driver and one boat handler, agree on a stop signal, and keep children and bystanders outside the trailer path. If the first approach is unstable, go around rather than forcing it.

Once clear, secure the bow, transom, motor and loose gear for road travel; connect and verify lights and brakes; inspect the coupler; and record any issue. The best first Fourth Lake day may cover very little water. It should leave the family with a known launch, a proven boat and more margin than it used.

How much trailer parking is listed?
