Myers Park With a Family Boat: Three Ramps, a Written Fee Gate and a Short Cayuga Lake Proof Run

The Town of Lansing publishes unusually useful launch facts. Use them to build a short first visit, not permission to outrun weather, water quality or the crew.

Editorial disclosure: Independent trip planning with no paid links. Official fees, seasons, facility status, water quality and weather can change. Check the Town of Lansing, New York DEC and National Weather Service immediately before towing.

Myers Park sits about ten miles north of Ithaca on Cayuga Lake’s east shore. The Town of Lansing publishes three paved launch ramps, a marina, picnic areas, pavilions, camping, a swimming area and playground. That makes it a strong family base—but not a guaranteed easy day. Cayuga Lake can build wind and fetch, launch traffic changes, harmful algal blooms are patchy and municipal fees or facilities can change. The smart first visit keeps the route close, the shore plan independent and the return early.

Read the two official fee gates before leaving home

The town’s current marina page publishes 2026 launch fees of $6 per launch for Lansing residents and $8 for nonresidents. It also publishes seasonal permits of $50 resident and $70 nonresident, with lower senior rates, and requires a trailer-registration copy for a seasonal permit. The Myers Park page separately publishes an $8 per-car admission charge for nonresidents.

Treat launch and park admission as separate possible charges and confirm both before towing. Ask what payment methods the launch office accepts, when the office is staffed, whether a seasonal permit changes the entry charge and how an after-hours launch is handled. Do not infer a current total from one page.

Stage the family away from the ramp lanes

Prepare food, PFDs, lines, fenders, drain plug, electronics and passenger assignments before approaching the ramp. The town code specifically prohibits riding in a boat, trailer or watercraft while it is being towed by a motor vehicle during launching or loading. Children stay in the vehicle or a designated safe shore position with their assigned adult—never in the boat on the trailer.

At the lane, do only the work required to launch. Move the tow vehicle promptly to legal parking. One adult controls the boat, one controls the vehicle and one controls children if staffing allows. If the crew cannot divide those jobs safely, practice at a quieter familiar ramp first.

Run a short proof loop before choosing a direction

After launch, stay near enough to return immediately while checking cooling flow, steering, throttle response, bilge, battery voltage, engine sound, PFD fit and passenger comfort. Observe flags, tree movement, whitecaps and traffic from the water. The lake’s long north-south shape means a modest breeze can create uncomfortable conditions.

Choose the first leg only after the proof. Favor the shoreline that leaves the simplest return for the observed wind, but keep safe water, charts and local restrictions in control. This guide does not prescribe a waypoint or promise travel time. Do not turn a first family visit into a crossing or a mileage target.

Use a three-hour first-visit itinerary

Minute 0–25: confirm office, fees, ramp, parking, facilities and water from shore; complete the crew brief. Minute 25–50: launch, park and run the mechanical proof close to the ramp. Minute 50–110: make one short reversible shoreline leg with the return continuously obvious.

Minute 110–145: return before bathroom pressure or afternoon wind owns the schedule. Minute 145–175: retrieve, clear the lane, tie down and complete Clean–Drain–Dry. Use remaining energy for a picnic or playground if open. These are planning allowances, not published park operating times.

Check harmful algal blooms twice

Review the New York HAB notifications map before departure, but DEC warns that the map is not comprehensive. Conditions can appear between reports and a bloom may be localized. Scan the ramp, shoreline and intended stop for discolored water, streaks, surface scum or suspicious mats.

Keep people and pets out of water with a suspected bloom and avoid boating through concentrated material when it can create spray. Do not treat clear-looking water elsewhere as proof that the entire lake is unaffected. Carry drinking water rather than relying on lake water for people or pets. The family HAB gate covers the stop rules.

Pack for independence even when facilities are listed

Bring properly fitted PFDs, weather layers, sun protection, drinking water, food, medications, first-aid kit, charged communications, paper backup, spare dry clothing and the boat’s required safety equipment. Pack a small shore bag so a launch cancellation still works.

Confirm current restroom, concession, swimming, camping and picnic status with the town. A feature listed for the park may be seasonal, closed or located farther from the ramp than a child can comfortably manage. Plan a bathroom stop before launch and set an early return trigger.

Use Salt Point as a separate shore fallback, not a motorboat launch

The town describes nearby Salt Point Natural Area as 34 acres immediately north of Myers Park, open daily from 7 a.m. to dark, with trails and free entry. Its page allows nonmotorized boat launching but directs motorboats and personal watercraft to Myers Park. Pets must be leashed, and restroom availability is seasonal.

That makes Salt Point a useful low-energy walk or shore observation stop when the family decides not to launch. It is not permission to move a motorboat launch there. Confirm parking and current site rules, keep children away from unprotected water edges and scan for HABs before any contact.

Ask exact accessibility and pet questions

For a passenger who needs an accessible route, call about the parking surface, grade to the ramp or dock, current boarding edge, gangway, restroom route, picnic surface and any seasonal removal. A municipal feature list does not prove that a particular passenger can board a particular boat at today’s water level. Use the transfer and seating question set where relevant.

Confirm current pet rules for the park, marina, swimming area and boat. Use a leash on shore where required, assign one handler and never let a pet enter suspicious water. A hot, crowded ramp may be a poor first pet outing even when pets are legally permitted.

Know who should skip Myers Park that day

Skip the launch when a gate is unanswered, official status is unclear, wind or storms exceed the operator’s margin, a visible bloom is present, the ramp or parking does not fit the rig, the crew cannot manage children away from the lanes, or a needed boarding feature is unconfirmed.

New operators should not use a busy three-lane facility as their first trailer practice. Large rigs should confirm turning and parking instead of assuming “three ramps” means unlimited space. Families who need guaranteed indoor space or food should build a separate land plan. A successful day is one that ends early with margin.

Retrieve before the whole crew is done

  1. Call the return while everyone can still execute a calm sequence.
  2. Recheck the ramp queue and choose a legal waiting position.
  3. Assign children to one adult away from the vehicle path.
  4. Land at idle speed and keep hands and feet out of pinch zones.
  5. Load only by the posted rules and trailer instructions.
  6. Clear the lane before securing gear and tie-downs.
  7. Drain legally and complete Clean–Drain–Dry before the next waterbody.
  8. Record the conditions, fees and facility facts that should change the next visit.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to launch at Myers Park?

The Town of Lansing marina page checked November 19, 2026 publishes $6 per launch for Lansing residents and $8 for nonresidents. The park page separately lists an $8 nonresident vehicle-admission fee. Confirm the current total and payment method.

How many ramps are at Myers Park?

The town publishes three paved launch ramps. Lane availability, water level, dock status, queues and trailer parking still require a same-day check.

Can I launch a motorboat at Salt Point?

The town directs motorboats and personal watercraft to Myers Park. Salt Point lists nonmotorized launching only.
