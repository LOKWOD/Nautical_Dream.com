A Free Vessel Safety Check: Arrive Prepared, Leave With a Correction Record

Make the visit useful before asking about the decal. Confirm access, gather questions and leave knowing what still needs attention.

Editorial disclosure: This is an independent preparation guide, not a Coast Guard publication or endorsement. The official service is free; links are unpaid. Local appointments and eligibility must be confirmed with the examiner. Nautical Dream does not take bookings or award decals.

By Nautical Dream Editorial DeskUpdated October 20, 202611 minute read

A free Vessel Safety Check is most useful when the owner arrives ready to discuss the actual boat, not when everyone rushes to acquire a sticker before the first holiday launch. A short preparation session can make equipment easier to locate, make uncertainties visible and prevent the appointment from becoming a search through damp lockers. The objective here is a clear conversation and a closed correction loop. It is not a fresh universal equipment checklist, a legal opinion or a claim that an examiner will diagnose every hidden defect.

Know what you are arranging

The Coast Guard Auxiliary's current public site describes free, non-punitive checks by certified examiners, with equipment review and guidance when deficiencies are found. It provides the official request route. A decal may follow a satisfactory examination; arranging an appointment is not receiving approval.

The published Vessel Safety Check Manual, COMDTINST M16796.8A, dated October 2014, describes the program's scope and limits. It does not make the visit a complete marine survey or an electronics-calibration service. Use the examiner's current instructions for present requirements rather than copying every old technical detail into a shopping list.

Give the examiner an appointment brief, not your life story

Start from the official Auxiliary website, then use its current request or local contact route. We cannot promise a particular volunteer, response time, inspection date or location. If the online route is unavailable, use the official organization's local-directory route rather than entering payment details into an unrelated “inspection booking” result.

Prepare a concise message containing the type and approximate size of boat, general location, whether it is afloat or ashore, a contact method and a few possible windows. Ask about eligibility and the location they can attend. Send personal identification or complete boat documents only when genuinely needed through an appropriate verified channel. Do not publish home addresses, HIN photographs or registration documents in a public comment thread.

Confirm the access plan before moving the boat

Ask which condition and location the examiner expects and who must attend. The published program guidance permits some ashore examinations, but that does not mean your driveway, lift, yard or trailer setup is acceptable for a particular appointment. Obtain permission from the marina or boatyard when needed. Do not tow or launch solely on the assumption that an examiner will be waiting.

Record the meeting point, access contact, gate arrangements, safe boarding plan and weather cancellation method. Ask for an access arrangement that works for everyone involved. A boat on blocks or a trailer needs a safe supported setup; the appointment is not permission to improvise stairs or climb a ladder carrying equipment. When safe access cannot be established, reschedule.

Use a 30-minute owner preparation session

  1. First ten minutes: gather the boat's current papers, relevant manuals and previous correction notes. Identify questions; do not assume a document's presence proves it is current or correct.
  2. Next ten minutes: make an equipment-location list from the boat's actual arrangement. Record where the relevant items are kept without moving them into less accessible storage.
  3. Final ten minutes: write the top three uncertainties, confirm the meeting and arrange enough time afterward to discuss next steps.

This is our planning allowance, not the examiner's service duration. Agree their expected time directly and leave additional margin for travel or marina access. If the boat's records are scattered, prepare over several short sessions rather than turning the morning into a hurried repair exercise. The logbook system can hold the resulting record.

Bring an equipment-location sheet that answers real questions

Create four columns: item, installed or stored location, document or label to discuss, and unresolved question. Useful discussion headings include flotation equipment, fire and distress equipment, lights, sound signals, documents and state-specific requirements. This list describes conversation topics, not the complete equipment required for every vessel or waterway.

For example, write “inflatable jacket: port locker; label and model available; ask about this user's suitability,” not “life jackets done.” Write down uncertainty about a label, date or missing manual rather than inventing its answer. The life-jacket selection guide, extinguisher guide and distress-signal guide are background reading, not a substitute for current official requirements.

Separate a requirement, a recommendation and a repair

Applicable requirementWhat current rule and boat or operating condition makes this apply?Specific deficiency and required correction
Safety recommendationWhat problem would this address on our actual outings?Priority, fit and the owner's decision
Technical concern outside the visitWhich qualified professional should assess it?Service referral and operating restriction until resolved

This is our note-taking matrix, not an official classification form. Ask the examiner to clarify the finding in their own terms. Do not mark an item “optional” because it appears under a recommendation heading in an older publication; a state or the actual operation may change what applies. Keep the finding and its source together.

Keep hazardous demonstrations out of owner preparation

Do not discharge a flare, release an extinguisher, transmit a distress call or defeat an engine cutoff device to show enthusiasm for the appointment. Follow the examiner's lawful instructions and the exact equipment manual. A school-age child can help organize blank questions at a kitchen table; the child should not handle pyrotechnics or operate boat systems for an adult's rehearsal.

Fuel odor, questionable wiring, structural damage or an unsafe boarding route needs the appropriate response before the appointment. Tell the examiner about a known concern and seek qualified service. The professional-repair boundary remains in force. A scheduled courtesy visit is not emergency assistance and does not justify starting a suspect engine to keep the meeting.

Close the loop with an action–owner–evidence record

Before the conversation ends, ask what must be corrected, who should do it and how follow-up verification should be arranged. Keep any official paperwork supplied. Beside each action, add an owner, target date, supporting manual or part reference and the evidence that will show completion. Leave unresolved items visibly open.

Do not buy every product mentioned before checking fit and the actual requirement. A replacement should solve the documented problem, not merely look newer. For a technical repair, retain the professional's work record and any operating restrictions. Ask the examiner directly what further visit or verification is needed; do not invent a recheck deadline or apply a decal yourself based on this article.

Treat the result as one layer, not a season-long clearance

A completed visit does not select the weather, inspect the next day's loading or teach a new operator. Continue the predeparture routine, weather decision and recall checks. Use a qualified marine surveyor or technician for the separate work they perform, including pre-purchase or condition concerns.

Skip or postpone an appointment with unsafe access, an unconfirmed meeting or a boat that needs urgent attention first. Bad weather can become a useful at-home records session; it is not a reason to hurry a launch. The lasting benefit is a better documented boat and a more informed owner, even when a correction remains open.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nautical Dream book the free check?

No. Arrange it through the official Auxiliary request or local contact route and confirm availability directly.

Does a decal replace a survey or guarantee every future trip?

No. Keep technical assessment, repairs, current legal requirements and daily operating decisions separate.

Must I launch for the appointment?

Ask the examiner. Some ashore examinations are possible, but the actual location and safe access must be agreed before moving the boat.
