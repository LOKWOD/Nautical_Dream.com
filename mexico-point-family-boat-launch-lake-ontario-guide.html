Mexico Point With a Family Boat: Treat the Little Salmon River as the Proof Zone Before Lake Ontario

A large ramp does not make a Great Lake small. Prove the boat and crew inside the Little Salmon River, then treat the lake opening as a second departure decision.

Mexico Point is a useful Lake Ontario gateway because it begins in the Little Salmon River, but that protected start is not evidence that the lake is suitable. State Parks publishes 24-hour boat launching from April 1 through November 1, a seasonal fee and restrooms; DEC publishes dual concrete ramps and capacity for 105 cars and trailers. Today is outside that stated launch window. Plan for the next verified open season, and call the facility before any off-season assumption.

Reconcile the two official records before relying on either

State Parks describes a large concrete launch off Route 104B on County Route 40, entering the Little Salmon River with close access to Lake Ontario. It publishes boat launching 24 hours a day from April 1 through November 1, a $7 car-and-trailer fee during that window, $18 overnight parking, restrooms, picnicking without fires and a two-pet policy subject to current signs.

DEC’s Oswego County directory and launch PDF identify hard-surface or dual concrete ramps and list 105 cars and trailers plus 29 cars. State Parks also warns that parking is limited. Treat the capacity number as a facility record, not a guarantee of an open legal space. Fees, dates and services are volatile: verify the official page and facility contact immediately before departure.

Stage the complete boat before entering the ramp lanes

Install the drain plug, load required equipment, remove transom tie-downs as directed, rig bow and stern lines and assign each adult a job in the staging area. Children wait in a named safe zone clear of the tow vehicle, trailer, winch line and water edge. Nobody stands behind the trailer or between vehicle and trailer.

Confirm how the automated pay station works before occupying a lane. At the water, stop for broken, slimed, undermined or submerged ramp edges that your tow vehicle or trailer cannot manage. Launch, clear the ramp and complete passenger settling away from traffic. Review the launch checklist and busy-ramp plan before arrival.

Make the Little Salmon River a systems proof—not a warm-up sprint

Use the sheltered portion for a low-speed proof loop while staying clear of traffic and obeying every posted rule. Verify steering response, control movement, cooling indication, bilge, battery voltage, depth trend, radio, crew seating and the visual route back to the ramp. Note current, debris, anglers and the amount of safe maneuvering room.

A successful first visit can end after this proof and retrieval. Do not run into Lake Ontario because the ramp queue is light or another boat continues. The operator should be able to state the forecast zone, return bearing, fuel reserve, communication plan and the condition that triggers an immediate turn before passing the river opening.

Treat Lake Ontario as a separate voyage decision

The NWS nearshore zone from Sodus Bay to Mexico Bay is LOZ044. Read the current nearshore forecast, active advisories and observed sky and water immediately before departure and again before leaving the river. A forecast wave height describes a statistical sea state, not every wave, wake or local condition. Wind direction, fetch, storms and shoreline effects can change the ride and the return.

Set limits for the actual boat, loading, operator and children; a generic wind or wave number is not a universal go/no-go rule. If the forecast, observed lake, clouds, visibility or crew confidence differs from the plan, stay in the river only if that remains lawful and safe—or retrieve. Pair the decision with the family weather plan.

Plan for cold water even on a sunny shoulder-season day

The official launch window reaches into spring and fall, when cold water can turn a routine overboard event into an emergency. Every person should wear a properly fitted, serviceable life jacket appropriate to the activity and conditions. Check the device label, child weight range and state rules; an adult device is not a child substitute.

Carry dry insulating layers in a protected bag, keep the crew seated, and avoid an itinerary that depends on swimming or wading. Never jump in after property. If a person enters the water, use the boat’s practiced recovery plan, call for help early and follow current Coast Guard guidance. Cold-water concerns are a reason to shorten or cancel, not a reason to improvise with extra clothing that defeats PFD fit.

Use real communication and navigation backups

Cell service is not a marine distress system. Carry a marine VHF appropriate to the vessel and know how to make a distress call; confirm registration and DSC details where installed. Keep a charged phone protected as a separate tool, but do not rely on an app as the only chart, weather source or route home.

Use current official charts and the boat’s depth and navigation equipment within their limitations. Assign a lookout and keep the river entrance identifiable from the lake. If the crew cannot reliably communicate with shore or another vessel, keep the route close enough to execute the written fallback without rescue assumptions. Review the VHF selection guide before shopping.

Carry a facility-independent family logistics plan

State Parks currently lists restrooms and allows picnicking without fires, but services can be seasonal, closed or changed. Use a restroom before arrival, carry drinking water and known snacks, and keep all waste in the tow vehicle. Do not refuel, reorganize gear or hold a family meeting on the active ramp.

Pets are limited by the current State Parks policy and signs; the published summary allows no more than two in day-use areas, supervised and crated or on a leash no longer than six feet, with proof of rabies vaccination available if requested. Verify the exact rules before bringing a dog and use the dog-aboard plan.

Retrieve while the river, ramp and family are still easy

Set a return time before launch and start back with fuel, daylight and adult attention in reserve. Reassess the river entrance, traffic, current, wind and queue. One adult owns the tow vehicle; another owns children. Load at idle speed, clear the lane and secure the boat for road travel in the staging area.

Follow New York’s current clean-drain-dry law and onsite instructions. Remove visible plants, animals and mud; drain where legal; never move water between waterbodies. Record ramp condition, queue, forecast zone, proof-loop time and the first turn-back cue for the next visit. The observations expire; the decision record remains useful.

Know who should skip Mexico Point

Skip this launch when the published season is closed; when the boat, trailer or operator has not been proven on a simpler site; when children cannot remain in assigned seats and fitted PFDs; when the crew needs guaranteed services beyond the current official record; or when the Lake Ontario plan lacks forecast, communication and a conservative return.

The fallback is not “try the lake and see.” Use a shore visit only where the current facility rules permit, or choose a protected inland waterbody and verified open launch. A river proof loop without lake entry is also a complete boating day when every gate passes.

Frequently asked questions

Is Mexico Point open for boat launching in November?

The current State Parks page publishes 24-hour boat launching from April 1 through November 1. November 17 is outside that window. Confirm directly with the facility before any off-season assumption.

Does 105 trailer spaces mean parking will be available?

No. DEC publishes that capacity, while State Parks warns that parking is limited. Current signs, closures, events and actual legal spaces control.

Is the Little Salmon River proof loop enough to clear the boat for Lake Ontario?

No. It can reveal boat and crew problems, but Lake Ontario requires a separate weather, sea-state, route, communication and return decision.
