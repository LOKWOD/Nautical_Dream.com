Point Au Roche State Park gives families a year-round Lake Champlain launch and a strong shore alternative, but the access record needs careful reading. DEC lists two Point Au Roche hard-surface launches with separate coordinates and parking counts, while the park page uses multiple entrances and publishes different seasonal services. Start with the exact launch—not just the park name

The park’s current page lists the boat launch open year-round, daily from sunrise to sunset. DEC’s Clinton County table separately lists a hard-surface Point Au Roche State Park launch with 21 car-and-trailer spaces and another Point Au Roche hard-surface launch with 40. Save the chosen coordinates and confirm the entrance with park staff instead of assuming both records lead to the same lane or parking plan.

DEC defines a hard-surface launch as providing float-off and float-on launching for most trailered boats. “Most” is not a fit guarantee. Ramp slope, water level, dock condition, tow vehicle, trailer geometry and boat draft still control. Ask which facility suits the actual rig and whether seasonal docks or lane restrictions are in effect.

Make broad-water weather the first go/no-go gate

NWS Burlington issues a dedicated Lake Champlain recreational forecast during the operating season. It distinguishes northern, broad and southern waters and explains that significant wave height is an average of the highest one-third of waves, so individual waves can be higher. Use the applicable lake forecast, current observations, radar and warnings, then compare them with the actual water at the launch.

Point Au Roche sits on the northwestern shore, but a protected ramp view does not prove the intended route is protected. Write a conservative family limit for wind direction, sustained wind, gusts, waves, visibility, thunderstorms, water temperature and the least capable passenger. Turn while the easy return remains easy. The large-water weather workflow and wind-and-fetch guide help frame the check without replacing the forecast.

Keep the first loop reversible

  1. Dry-side stage: fit life jackets, assign adult roles, secure gear and save the exact return point.
  2. Launch and clear: move the tow vehicle legally without leaving children in the traffic path.
  3. Systems proof: verify steering, cooling indication, bilge, controls, communication and crew comfort near return.
  4. Short out-and-back: extend only inside current charts, weather and the operator’s demonstrated limits.
  5. Early recovery: retrieve before fatigue or changing wind consumes the margin.

This is a decision sequence, not a route. Use current navigation information and local knowledge. Do not infer a safe path from the conceptual illustration or from another boat’s track.

Do not plan around unavailable 2026 moorings

The park page says mooring lines were not available for the 2026 season. Treat that as a hard planning constraint. Do not promise a lunch stop, overnight hold or beach arrival that depends on a mooring. If a future page lists moorings again, confirm the current rules, availability, fee and how the system is intended to be used before departure.

Anchoring is a separate seamanship and legal decision requiring current charts, sufficient room, appropriate equipment, bottom and weather knowledge, and a practiced crew. A missing mooring is not permission to improvise an anchorage. Keep food, rest and child-care needs workable aboard or after recovery.

Use the park as a real shore backup

The official page lists picnic facilities, trails, a playground, swimming beach and Nature Center. Each has its own season, hours and access conditions. The shore plan can be a nature-center visit, picnic and short trail walk after the boat remains on the trailer. Confirm holiday closures—the page specifically lists the Nature Center closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas—and check whether the desired restroom or beach service is operating.

A backup works only when the family wants to use it. Pack walking shoes, dry layers, lunch and a simple activity that does not depend on the water. Do not keep children waiting at the ramp while the operator debates a marginal forecast.

Treat accessibility and pet access as task-specific

The park lists an ADA-accessible kayak launch and accessible amenities, but that does not prove a particular passenger can transfer to a private boat at either trailer ramp. Ask about parking surface, route, dock height, edge protection, restroom and the current relationship between dock and water. Use only boarding equipment already fitted and practiced for the boat.

The current pet policy allows up to two supervised pets in permitted day-use areas, crated or on a leash no longer than six feet, with proof of rabies vaccination available if requested. Pets are excluded from listed areas including guarded beaches and buildings unless an applicable service-animal rule applies. Confirm posted directions and use the dog boarding and heat plan for the boat.

Complete Clean, Drain and Dry at both ends

DEC’s Clinton County page names several invasive species in Lake Champlain and states that New York law requires Clean, Drain and Dry steps before launching into public waters. Inspect the hull, trailer, bunks, axle area, propeller, anchor, lines, livewell and fishing gear. Remove plants and mud, drain where lawful and dry before moving to another waterbody.

Spiny waterflea can travel in water and fishing gear even when no plant is visible. Follow the current DEC instructions rather than a generic rinse ritual. Keep the transfer record with the tow rig so the next lake does not depend on memory.

Know who should choose another day

Skip this plan when the exact launch cannot be identified; the ramp, dock or parking status is uncertain; the NWS lake forecast exceeds the boat or crew’s conservative limit; ice, fog, thunder or cold-water exposure removes the safe margin; the family requires a mooring or guaranteed beach; or the operator is not ready for Lake Champlain’s scale and traffic.

A shore visit is a successful day. A smaller protected water with a simpler recovery may be the better boating plan. Compare Lake George family planning, Great Sacandaga Lake, a Thousand Islands first trip and the family weather gate.
