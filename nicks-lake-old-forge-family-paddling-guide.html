Nicks Lake Near Old Forge With Kids: No-Motor Water, Seasonal Launch and a Two-Hour Proof Loop

Nicks Lake rewards a quiet small-craft plan. The launch is seasonal, motors are prohibited, and the best first objective is a short loop that keeps the landing obvious.

Editorial disclosure: Independent trip-planning guidance with no paid links. The launch, campground, fee, beach supervision, parking, amenities, water level, weather and operating dates can change. NYSDEC, posted signs and on-site staff control the day. The images are conceptual and do not identify Nicks Lake.

Nicks Lake is a 205-acre shallow Adirondack lake near Old Forge where NYSDEC prohibits motor boats. DEC lists an unimproved trailer launch, twenty-car parking and a day-use fee in season, with access only while the campground operates. That makes it a canoe, kayak or rowboat day with a seasonal gate—not a place to arrive after hours with a motor attached and improvise.

Start with the seasonal gate, not the route

The official Nicks Lake page currently lists an opening date of May 15 and closing date of October 12. That window has ended as of November 8, 2026 and must not be copied into a future trip without checking the live page. The Herkimer County launch listing says the launch is open when the campground is operating and charges a day-use fee in season.

Before driving, call the campground number on the official page and confirm the opening date, day-use arrival window, payment method, launch condition, beach status, restroom availability and any closure. A campsite reservation is separate from day use. Build the trip with the destination decision gate, not an old screenshot.

Respect the no-motor rule literally

DEC says no motor boats are allowed, and the launch directory says no motors permitted. Do not infer that an electric trolling motor is acceptable because it is quiet, small or not in use. Confirm whether even a mounted, raised or disconnected motor is permitted before transport. The conservative plan is a paddle- or oar-powered craft with no motor aboard.

Match craft choice to the weakest paddler, wind and recovery plan. A canoe may carry family gear efficiently but needs practiced teamwork. Sit-on-top kayaks simplify some exits but can expose children to cold spray. A rowboat changes seating and propulsion. Review the backup paddle plan and never exceed the craft capacity label.

Build the trip around a two-hour proof loop

0:00–0:25: park only where directed, walk the launch and assign carry roles before unloading. Keep children out of the vehicle lane and shoreline work zone. Stage craft above the water, fit life jackets and load only after the landing route is clear. Use the no-courtesy-dock launch system even when the site feels quiet.

0:25–0:40: run a ten-minute near-shore proof loop. Test seating, foot braces, paddle reach, steering, communication and the return landing. 0:40–1:35: choose a shoreline out-and-back that preserves an easy turn. 1:35–2:00: land before fatigue, carry craft clear and finish with a 15-minute reset.

Treat shallow water as a planning fact, not a promise

DEC describes Nicks Lake as shallow. Shallow does not mean uniformly warm, weed-free, wadeable or easy to recover from. Bottom material, submerged wood, rocks, aquatic plants and drop-offs can vary. Do not let children walk an unknown bottom beside a loaded boat, and do not assume a capsized paddler can stand.

Keep fitted life jackets worn, secure footwear, and maintain a close-to-shore route that still avoids swimmers, anglers and habitat. Do not drag hulls across vegetation. If weeds foul a paddle or rudder, return to a safe landing rather than leaning overboard. The water-level launch gate provides the right hold/leave logic.

Check weather for the lake, not Old Forge plans

Open the National Weather Service point forecast and hourly wind before leaving, then check again at the lake. Record sustained wind, gusts, thunder timing, temperature and precipitation. A sheltered launch can hide more exposed water. Turn around at the first meaningful loss of control; do not use the far shore as shelter unless it is a lawful landing.

Thunder ends the water plan. Cold air and water reduce dexterity and make wet children deteriorate quickly. Use the family weather ladder and cold-layer life-jacket fit. The schedule is expendable. If the forecast depends on threading a narrow window, use the shore fallback.

Keep swimming and boating plans separate

The Nicks Lake facility page describes a large beach with lifeguards, while DEC’s current statewide campground-rules page says campground swimming is unsupervised by lifeguards. Treat that conflict as a stop-and-confirm item, not permission to assume coverage. Call staff, read the current signs and use only the designated beach while it is officially open; DEC rules prohibit swimming from one-half hour before sunset until one-half hour after sunrise.

A launch is not a swimming beach, and beach supervision does not make the rest of the lake guarded. Transition deliberately: land and secure the craft, change supervision roles, account for every child and follow posted rules. If the beach is closed, do not create an unofficial swim. The swimming-from-a-boat plan is not a substitute for local rules.

Pack for carry, food, pets and bathroom reality

Bring worn fitted PFDs, spare paddle, whistle, waterproof communication, route card, first-aid kit, dry clothing, sun protection, water, simple food, trash container and a small repair kit permitted by the craft maker. Keep the load below the craft limit and balanced. A cart helps only if the current path allows it and the cart can be controlled.

Confirm restrooms on the day and have a leave plan if they are closed. DEC’s facility page identifies six accessible campsites and five accessible shower buildings, but that does not establish an accessible boat transfer or launch route; call staff about the exact access need. For pets, DEC requires current rabies proof, a leash no longer than six feet and control at all times, and prohibits pets on beaches, in picnic areas and in buildings. Confirm local details before bringing one.

Keep food secured from wildlife and follow DEC carry-in/carry-out rules. The food-safety packing guide and family first-aid system prevent a short paddle from becoming a loose-gear expedition.

Use the shore fallback without calling the day a failure

If the launch is closed, full, rough or unsuitable, keep the family out of the unloading lane and switch to the confirmed day-use option. The official page lists hiking, a picnic area, playground and other recreation, but availability and rules must be checked. Do not wander onto a closed beach or campsite as a substitute.

A low-energy version is lunch, the accessible facility check and one short walk within the open area. A weather version is a brief shore observation followed by Old Forge. Keep children away from wet rocks, launch traffic and unattended water. The day succeeds when the family leaves before conditions or patience collapse.

Know who should skip Nicks Lake

Skip the paddle when the campground is closed, staff cannot confirm access, the crew needs a motor, the craft cannot be carried or controlled at the unimproved launch, wind exceeds the weakest paddler’s limit, thunder threatens, or cold-water gear and recovery skills are inadequate. Also skip when the only adult cannot supervise children while handling the craft.

Families seeking a full-service marina, rental guarantee or broad powerboating water should choose another destination. Nicks Lake’s value is its no-motor quiet and reversible small-craft plan. Respect that limit and the day stays simple.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an electric trolling motor on Nicks Lake?

Do not assume so. NYSDEC says no motors permitted. Confirm any question about a mounted or disconnected motor directly with campground staff before transport.

Is the Nicks Lake launch open year-round?

No. DEC says the launch is available when the campground operates. Current dates and conditions must be confirmed before travel.

Does the campground guarantee a lifeguarded beach?

No. Beach status and lifeguard coverage are operational details that can change. Check the official page, call staff and obey posted signs.
